# The interactive tour

*A plan, 12 September 2026. Shane: a second version of the review that is interactive —
much less prose, more visuals, diagrams, tables and little demos, a "click to load a
client-side simulation of what this section discusses". A tour of the museum, of the
browser client, of how it works and how it got working, and of how Counter-Strike 1.6
itself works — protocol differences, anything that can be shown. Not all-encompassing
yet; complementing the prose review or merged with it. Server impact minimal. Plan first.*

## What it is

A page of **stops**. Each stop is a title, two or three sentences, one visual — a diagram,
a table, a screenshot, a short clip — and, where the subject allows it, a **widget**: a
thing the visitor can drag, drop, click or watch, running in their browser. Every stop
carries a link into the prose review's matching section, so the tour is the short, visual
layer and the review stays the long read. Nothing loads that the visitor did not ask for:
the whole first wave should cost a visitor under two megabytes, and the engine (202 MB,
cached after the first time) sits only behind a click that says so.

The tour is also the museum's first wing. An exhibit is precisely a visual, a little
interaction and a record; the widgets built for the tour — a map fly-through, a model
turntable, a demo inspector, a generated clip — are the components an artifact's page is
made of, and the collection in the room supplies the objects. So this is not a side
document: it is the museum's page framework, built on the story we already have to tell.

## The shape

- **Where:** a Vite entry beside the play page, `web/tour.html` with `web/tour/`, built to
  `dist/tour/` and served by the relay at `/tour`. Same toolchain, same login for now.
- **Stops as data:** `web/tour/stops.ts` lists them — id, title, text, the visual, the
  widget if any, the review anchor. Adding a stop is adding an entry; the framework draws
  the page, the table of contents and the links.
- **Widgets as modules:** `web/tour/widgets/<name>.ts`, each a function that takes a
  container and returns nothing the page needs to know about. They read only what the
  site already serves: `manifest.json`, `catalogue.json`, `/api/demos`, `/raw` for a map's
  files. No new endpoints in the first wave.
- **Visuals made by the harness:** the screenshots and clips come from the bench
  (Playwright drives the play page, the demo page and, later, the tour itself), so a
  visual is regenerated with a command, never pasted. `bench/tour.mjs` makes them all.
- **Diagrams:** hand-drawn SVG in a small TS helper for the handful we need (boxes,
  arrows, a timeline). Mermaid would cost every visitor a megabyte for the same picture.
- **The review:** unchanged for now. Its section ids are the tour's link targets. When
  the tour covers most of it, its sections fold in as "the long read" under each stop and
  `/review` redirects; that is the merge, and it happens stop by stop rather than in one
  move.

## The first wave: six stops that prove the format

| stop | what the visitor sees | the widget | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. What you are looking at** | the system map: browser engine ↔ WebRTC data channel ↔ relay ↔ UDP ↔ ReHLDS in Docker ↔ the room | hover a box for what it is; a button measures this browser's round trip to the relay, live | 0.5 day |
| **2. The lag is not the network** | §7's finding as a chart | a netcode simulator on a canvas: a moving target, the snapshots the server sends (update rate 20–100), what the client draws (interpolating behind by `ex_interp` or one update), ping and loss as sliders; the visible delay printed as it changes | 1 day |
| **3. Inside a recording** | the anatomy of a `.dem`: header, directory, the two sections, the frame kinds | drop a demo or pick one of ours: the page parses the header, directory and frame types in the browser (a small port of `relay/demoinfo.go`'s walk, no delta decoding needed), draws the section timeline and the frame-type histogram, lists what the recorder typed; "play it" goes to `/demos/<name>` | 1 day |
| **4. Protocol 46, 47, 48** | the timeline 1998 → 2003 → 2008 and a byte-by-byte layout of the messages that differ, the differences highlighted | pick a protocol and the layout re-draws; the table from the demo proposal | 0.5 day |
| **5. Every map and what it needs** | the catalogue as a table with red for missing, filterable | click a map: a fly-through in hlviewer.js from the server's files (a few MB, on request), beside its record | 1 day |
| **6. Watch a game** | a thirty-second muted clip of a real recording, generated by the bench | click for the full demo page, with the download cost stated | 0.5 day |

Plus the framework itself, one day: the page, the stops file, the SVG helper, the bench
that makes the visuals, the relay route. About five and a half days for a tour that has
something to drag, something to drop a file on, something to fly through, something to
watch, and one live measurement.

## The second wave, when the first has been used

- **How the engine got here:** the timeline — the 2025 port, its deletion in August 2026,
  the recovery, the build from source — and the six patches as cards, each with its
  before-and-after number (the heap, the fragment buffer, the hidden tab's frames).
- **The game in bundles:** what this browser has cached, read from IndexedDB; click a map
  for its bundle.
- **A hidden tab:** the measured frame rates before and after patch 0004 as a chart, and
  a live readout of this tab's own frame clock.
- **A packet's journey:** an animated diagram of one datagram, and why every browser
  gets a loopback address of its own.
- **The museum's records:** the record shape, the collection's numbers, and the first
  artifact pages built from the same widgets.
- **HLTV:** the picture from the discussion of 11 September, once it exists.
- **How Counter-Strike works**, a growing set: delta compression shown as a packet
  shrinking; the delta descriptions a server sends; what a usercmd is; the buy menu as
  user messages; anything the demo inspector can point at.

## Decisions for Shane

1. **A separate `/tour`, converging on the review by shared section ids** (recommended),
   or rebuild the review in place from the start. Separate lets the first wave ship in a
   week without touching a document that works.
2. **Audience.** Behind the family login like everything else, or built public-safe from
   the start — no `/raw`, no live measurement — so it can become the front page. The
   first wave can be either; the map fly-through is the one stop that needs `/raw`.
3. **The first six.** Swap any for a second-wave stop; the netcode simulator and the demo
   inspector are the two I would keep whatever else changes, because they are the ones
   that show how the game works rather than how ours does.
4. **Name.** `/tour` now; `/museum` with wings — the story, the maps, the recordings, the
   machinery — when the collection's pages join it.

## Built, 12 September 2026

Shane's decisions: a separate `/tour`, borrowing from the review later; the family login
for now, a front page eventually; and the finished order top down — what Counter-Strike
is, its history, this project and why, the maps, the models and skins, the engine, the
network, the patches, the extras, and (his afterthought, and the museum's rule) the
credits and archives. The first pass has all ten chapters in that order, each with a
few sentences and at least one real visual or widget:

1. **What Counter-Strike is** — thirty seconds of a real recording, captured from the
   demo page's own canvas by the bench (`bench/tour.mjs clip`, a `MediaRecorder` on the
   canvas so the loading screen is not in it; 5 MB of webm, no ffmpeg on the machine);
   the round, in words.
2. **A short history** — the timeline, 1996 to now, hover for each event; gold is ours.
3. **This project, and why** — the system map, hover a box; a button that measures this
   browser's round trip to the relay.
4. **The maps** — the catalogue as a filterable table, red for missing; fly through any
   map in hlviewer.js, from the server's files, beside its record.
5. **The models and skins** — an inspector that reads a `.mdl` header in the browser:
   bones, sequences, body parts, and the textures drawn from their palettes; opens on a
   model the server can serve, takes a dropped file.
6. **The engine** — the parts the browser downloads, measured live with a HEAD request
   each; the bundles, and what this browser has cached, read from IndexedDB.
7. **The network** — a packet's journey, animated; the netcode simulator (update rate,
   `ex_interp`, ping, loss, and the delay you see); protocol 46, 47, 48 as byte layouts
   that redraw for the protocol picked.
8. **The patches** — six cards, each with the number it moved.
9. **Extras** — the recording inspector: header, directory, frames by kind, what the
   recorder typed, the sounds their client played, and the path they walked drawn from
   the view origin every frame carries; opens on the smallest of ours, takes a drop.
10. **Credits and archives** — every name a link, the archives, the licences.

Chapters are entries in `web/tour/main.ts`, widgets modules in `web/tour/widgets/`,
each mounted when scrolled near. The relay serves `dist/tour.html` at `/tour`. A visitor
downloads the page, the clip and whatever they click; the engine only behind a link that
states its cost. `bench/tour.mjs shots` screenshots every chapter, which is how this pass
was checked.

Second wave, in the order Shane's structure suggests: the round as a diagram; the
engine's boot as measured steps; a hidden tab's frame rates; the model turntable
(web-hlmv); the collection's records as pages built from the same widgets; HLTV when it
exists; the deep dives into how the game works (delta compression, usercmds, the delta
descriptions a server sends).

## Extras, and the roadmap — 12 September 2026

Shane: a roadmap of what is next, in the tour's extras, and what else extras could hold.
Extras is now the deep-dive chapter — the parts that do not fit the story above, each on
real files, all of it in the browser:

- **Inside a recording**, now on a shared reader (`web/tour/dem.ts`, a port of
  `relay/demoinfo.go`): a dropped file gets the server's own greeting, the recorder, what
  was precached and the mod's user messages, not just frame counts.
- **Delta compression** — the one that earns the chapter. The reader decodes the
  `svc_deltadescription` messages the server sent, so the tables are that server's own:
  every field of `entity_state_player_t` with its type, bit width and divisor, what a
  running player actually changes, and the arithmetic (534 bits if everything were sent,
  156 for a real update, 71% saved, 0.9 KB/s per player not sent).
- **Inside a map file** — the BSP's fifteen lumps by size, the entity lump parsed, and
  the vertex lump drawn from above as the map's floor plan, with spawns as dots and the
  brush entities (bomb sites, buy zones, rescue zones) placed from the models lump as
  boxes. de_dust2: 101 entities, 6,555 vertices, both sites where they should be.
- **What your keyboard becomes** — a live `usercmd`: hold the keys, watch the fields and
  the sixteen button bits, with the note that the wire carries it delta-encoded to ten or
  twenty bits.
- **The sounds** — thirteen of the stock sounds, played from the server's own files.
- **What is next** — the roadmap: now, next, later, each item saying where it is written
  down in full, and a line of what has shipped.

**What else extras could hold**, in rough order of how well it would show:
