The Counter-Strike 1.6 museum

A museum of the 2003 game, grown out of a family server: the maps, the models and the recordings of the way it was played, kept with their histories and playable — the whole game runs in the browser tab, nothing to install. Everyone who comes in can look, try things, and say what they think; the curators just have a few more buttons.

The collection4,936 maps, 4,803 models, 48 recordings — by room, by rating, by tag. Look, fly through, play it on the lab, say what it is worth. PlayThe family server. Pick a name, join; twenty game types, changed from the lobby. The tourWhat Counter-Strike is, how this works, and the deep dives — with the real files.

Behind the family login for now. Open the site from your own invitation link and what you rate, tag or note is kept under your name. The first visit downloads the game (about 230 MB) and keeps it; a map you have not got comes down from the server as you join.

The serverThe one everybody plays on. One game type at a time — classic, arena, deathmatch, gungame, scoutz, climb, zombie, hide and seek, jailbreak and the rest — changed from the lobby by a curator.

How it works

Three pieces. The game server is the ordinary Half-Life dedicated server that ran every CS 1.6 server for twenty years, in a container. The client is the open-source Xash3D engine compiled to WebAssembly, with the Counter-Strike game code on top. Between them a small relay turns the browser's WebRTC packets into the UDP the server expects, and serves these pages. A map or a model the browser lacks comes from the server the game's own way. The tour draws it; the review explains it.
