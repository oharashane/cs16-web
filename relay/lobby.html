The Counter-Strike 1.6 museum

What is this? A museum of a 2003 computer game — the one where five terrorists and five counter-terrorists fought over a bomb or a few hostages, round after round, in internet cafés and on family PCs the world over. Everything in it is the real thing: the game runs in this browser tab, the maps and models are the files people made and passed around, the recordings are matches as they were played. Nothing to install.

It grew out of one family's server. Behind a family login for now; open it from your own invitation and what you rate is kept under your name. The first visit fetches the game itself — a few minutes on an ordinary connection — and keeps it.

How it works, in one breath

The game is Xash3D FWGS (the open engine that runs Half-Life's games) built to WebAssembly with cs16-client; it talks to a real Counter-Strike server (ReHLDS) through a small relay that turns the browser's WebRTC into the game's UDP. The maps, models and recordings are the files themselves, catalogued with their histories. The engine room has all of it, with the long read — the review, section by section, every measurement — at the foot of it and of the story.

Counter-Strike and Half-Life are Valve's. The maps, models and recordings belong to whoever made them and are here as fan content, credited where the files or GameBanana say who.
